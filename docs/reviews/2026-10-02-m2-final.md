# Final review of M2 (sign-off S7)

Date: 2026-10-02, after sign-off runs S1-S5 on the real system (S6, the
soak, deferred). Three reviewers (AI agents in their own clones, own
`sc` builds and throwaway stores; the real scd, store and `/etc` were
not touched): security, data integrity across store + watcher + CLI, and
operations (long-run behaviour, standing in for the deferred soak).
Claude reproduced each finding before acting on it.

**Gate: no high-severity finding.** Two medium findings: one fixed, one
deferred. The rest are low and deferred (list below).

**Held up:**
- About 1,000 restores and 600 snapshots ran in parallel against 4 to 8
  writers using every edit pattern. There was no "database is locked",
  and no restore result differed from the disk. After settling, every
  path's newest row matched the disk, with no duplicate rows.
- 24 random `kill -9`s of the watcher (mid-batch, mid-rescan, in a
  3,000-file burst) and a `kill -9` of `sc restore` between rename and
  commit. The store stayed consistent every time.
- A real kernel overflow never gave a false `did not exist`. The M1 → M2
  migration and `sc-m1` on the migrated store both work.
- Real store: 0700 directory, every file 0600 root. Hard links to secrets
  are refused (`protected_hardlinks`). Restore through a symlinked parent,
  over a directory, or of a deleted row onto a directory is refused.
  Journal lines are quoted. A 200,000-name `.ssh` costs 11 MB and 0.9 s
  per rescan.
- Real system since install:
  - every one of 2,117 rows is explained;
  - the newest row of all 1,723 paths matches the disk;
  - no err lines; memory 11-18 MB;
  - the first hourly rescan took 0.37 s CPU and wrote 0 rows;
  - a normal day on this desktop adds about 0 rows (manifest comparison
    from 28 Sep to 2 Oct).

## Not changed

- Any user can rewrite `/etc/passwd` (`chfn`, `passwd`), and each change
  asks for a rescan of the home roots (at most one per 10 s) and stores a
  passwd blob. This is not tested on the real system; it is bounded by
  the rescan gap.
