## Round 5 (R5-1): signal handling, second version

- The whole snapshot or restore command, including printing its result, counts as running: a signal then never makes `sc` exit on its own with a message that could contradict the result (signals 2, paths 2).
- A second signal forces an exit only if no restore in this process has passed its point of no return; once a rename may have happened, `sc` keeps waiting (bounded: after the first signal it makes at most one more COMMIT attempt) and reports the truth (signals 1, paths 1).
- After its one line, `sc` ends by the signal it received (SIGINT, SIGTERM, SIGHUP), so a shell loop stops like it does for any other program; SIGQUIT and the other stop signals end with exit status 1 (signals 3). This is a deliberate exception to "exit 1 on any error": an interrupt is the user's request, and shells rely on it.
- SIGABRT, SIGTRAP, SIGSYS and kill-sent SIGILL/SIGBUS/SIGFPE/SIGSEGV are handled like SIGQUIT: cleanup, one line, exit 1, no stack trace (signals 4).
- MILESTONES.md and the handler comment describe the real contract (paths 3).
